Long-form pickSubstack · Aug 13, 2026

AI #181: Astra goes cyber-critical — and what OpenAI didn't say

On Aug 7, OpenAI disclosed that internal testing of its next model, Astra, showed cyber-offense capability strong enough that it couldn't rule out crossing its own "Critical" risk threshold — the first time a frontier lab has publicly slowed a release specifically over cybersecurity concerns. In this Aug 13 analysis, Zvi Mowshowitz digs into what OpenAI actually disclosed versus what it withheld, and argues the real test isn't the pause itself but whether Astra later ships with materially different safeguards — or whether "pausing" quietly becomes standard pre-release theater.

Anthropic will watermark all AI-generated text

New Claude models will embed invisible, machine-readable watermarks in generated text and C2PA provenance data in images/audio/video, complying with the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency rules — and applying the markers worldwide, not just for EU users.

TechCrunch · Axios

Apple trained its own AI model for China, with Alibaba's help

Breaking from reliance on third-party Chinese models, Apple built a proprietary LLM for its China-market Apple Intelligence features. Alibaba's Qwen powers the integration; Apple is reportedly the first foreign company approved to offer a proprietary AI model there.

MacRumors · Benzinga

Stanford/Arc Institute's AI-designed viruses reignite the governance-gap debate

A Science study (Aug 6) showed genome model Evo 2 designed 16 novel bacteriophages that killed E. coli, some outperforming natural viruses. The model is free and open-source; Johns Hopkins biosecurity experts say DNA-synthesis screening can't yet detect AI-generated sequences like these.

Google ships Gemini 3.7 Flash, its cheapest coding model yet

Launched Aug 13 at half the price of its predecessor and tuned for agentic coding workflows, arriving while the flagship Gemini 3.5 Pro remains delayed — a workhorse release, not a flagship one.

Axios · Bloomberg

Uber and Pony.ai to deploy 2,000+ robotaxis across Europe

Expanding beyond their Zagreb pilot to four more European cities, with Middle East deployment also planned — though the companies haven't named cities or given a timeline yet.

Long-form pickAl Jazeera · Aug 14, 2026

Russian ballistic attacks intensify as Kyiv claims a Dnipropetrovsk advance

Zelenskyy says Ukrainian forces completed an eight-month operation reclaiming 745 sq km and 26 settlements in Dnipropetrovsk, killing or wounding roughly 16,000 Russian troops (ISW's independent, geolocation-based estimate is more conservative — at least 627 sq km). But the claim lands alongside intensifying Russian missile strikes on Ukrainian cities, increasingly unintercepted as Ukraine's Patriot stockpile runs low, and reports that North Korea is moving up to 120 ballistic missiles into Russia's Voronezh region. Read together, it's less a story of momentum in one direction than both sides escalating simultaneously on different fronts.
